As of September 5, 2026, the Japan Meteorological Agency (JMA) has issued severe warnings for Miyazaki Prefecture due to the influence of Typhoon No. 24. The storm is bringing heavy rainfall and unstable atmospheric conditions to the region, with significant risks of landslides, flooding, and high waves expected to persist through September 7.

## What travelers need to know

Heavy rain is expected to continue across Miyazaki Prefecture, with the most severe conditions forecasted for the southern plains, northern plains, and southern mountainous areas. The JMA has specifically warned of a high risk of landslides in these areas starting from the evening of September 5. 

In addition to heavy rain, travelers should be aware of the following risks:
- **Flooding and Inundation:** Potential for localized flooding in low-lying areas.
- **Severe Weather:** The risk of lightning strikes and tornadoes associated with unstable air.
- **Coastal Conditions:** High waves accompanied by swells are expected along the coast, with wave heights reaching up to 3 meters.

## What you should do

- **Monitor Official Updates:** Check the [JMA website](https://www.jma.go.jp/) regularly for the latest warnings and the "Kikikuru" (Landslide Alert) system for real-time risk levels in your specific area.
- **Prioritize Safety:** If you observe signs of developing cumulonimbus clouds or severe weather, move indoors immediately.
- **Check Transport:** Expect potential delays or cancellations for local trains, buses, and flights. Check with your specific transport operators before heading to stations or airports.
- **Follow Local Guidance:** Adhere to any instructions or evacuation orders issued by local municipal authorities.

## What is confirmed so far

- **Landslide Risk:** A strict warning for landslides is in effect for the southern plains, northern plains, and southern mountainous areas of Miyazaki.
- **Rainfall Forecast:** Heavy, localized rain is expected to continue through September 7. 24-hour rainfall totals could reach up to 200mm in some areas between September 6 and September 7.
