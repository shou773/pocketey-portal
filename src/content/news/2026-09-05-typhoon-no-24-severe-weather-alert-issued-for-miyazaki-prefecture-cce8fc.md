The Japan Meteorological Agency (JMA) has issued a severe weather alert for Miyazaki Prefecture as Typhoon No. 24 brings unstable atmospheric conditions to the region. Authorities are urging strict vigilance for landslides, particularly in the southern plains, northern plains, and southern mountainous areas, starting from the evening of September 5.

## What travelers need to know

Heavy, localized rain accompanied by thunderstorms is expected to continue through September 7. The JMA warns that conditions may reach levels requiring formal weather warnings for heavy rain, flooding, and inundation. 

In addition to rainfall, coastal areas are experiencing high waves accompanied by swells. Travelers should also be aware of the risk of lightning strikes and sudden gusts, including potential tornadoes.

### Forecasted Rainfall
Significant 24-hour rainfall totals are expected across the prefecture:
- **September 5 to September 6:** Up to 150mm in the southern and northern plains and southern mountainous areas; 120mm in northern mountainous areas.
- **September 6 to September 7:** Up to 200mm across all regions.
- **September 7 to September 8:** Up to 120mm across all regions.

Coastal areas (southern and northern plains) should expect wave heights of up to 3 meters through September 6.

## What you should do

- **Monitor local updates:** Check the [JMA website](https://www.jma.go.jp/) frequently for the latest bulletins and the "Kikikuru" (Risk Maps) for real-time landslide and flood risk data.
- **Prioritize safety:** If you observe signs of developing cumulonimbus clouds, move indoors immediately to ensure your safety.
- **Check transport:** Expect potential delays or cancellations for regional transport services. Contact your airline, train operator, or accommodation provider directly to confirm the status of your travel plans.
- **Follow local guidance:** Adhere strictly to any instructions or evacuation guidance provided by local municipal authorities.

## What is confirmed so far

As of late September 4, the typhoon was located approximately 200 kilometers west of Amami City, moving south at about 20 km/h. Significant rainfall has already been recorded in the region since September 2, with areas such as Tsuno and Miyazaki City (Tano) recording over 380mm of precipitation. The next official update from the Miyazaki Local Meteorological Office is expected around 5:30 AM on September 5.
