The Japan Meteorological Agency (JMA) has issued formal heavy rain and landslide warnings for the Amami region in Kagoshima Prefecture. These warnings are currently in effect as of September 5, 2026, and are being monitored due to the potential for severe weather impacts.

## What travelers need to know

Formal warnings for heavy rain, specifically citing risks of landslides and flooding, are in place for several areas within the Amami region, including the southern part of the region, the village of Toshima, and the towns of Wadomari, China, and Yoron. 

In addition to the heavy rain warnings, the JMA has issued advisories for strong winds, high waves (with swells), and lightning across the Amami region. 

Note: The JMA has indicated that for the village of Toshima and the town of Yoron, the criteria for issuing landslide warnings have been temporarily lowered due to previous seismic activity in those areas. This means that warnings may be issued at lower rainfall thresholds than usual.

## What you should do

*   **Monitor local updates:** Check the [JMA website](https://www.jma.go.jp/) or local municipal disaster prevention broadcasts for the most current information.
*   **Verify travel plans:** If you are in or traveling to the Amami region, contact your transport operators (airlines, ferry services) to confirm if services are operating as scheduled, as weather conditions may cause delays or cancellations.
*   **Prioritize safety:** Follow the instructions of local authorities and avoid areas prone to landslides or flooding. If you are in an area under a warning, stay indoors and away from hazardous terrain.

## What is confirmed so far

As of the latest bulletin, the JMA has confirmed the following status for the Amami region:

*   **Heavy Rain Warnings:** In effect for the southern Amami region, Toshima Village, and the towns of Wadomari, China, and Yoron.
*   **Advisories:** Strong wind, high wave, and lightning advisories are active across the entire Amami region.
*   **Forecasted Conditions:** Maximum wind speeds of 15 m/s and wave heights of 4 meters are expected in affected areas through September 6.
